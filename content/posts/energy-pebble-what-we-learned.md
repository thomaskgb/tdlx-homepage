We put [Energy Pebbles](/projects/energy-pebble/) in about 10 households for several weeks and surveyed users at the start, weekly, and at the end. Here is what actually happened: the good, the messy, and the surprising.

## People actually looked at it
The most common response was: "I look at it every time I walk past." We placed them in kitchens, hallways, near the front door, in high-traffic spots. Multiple people reported checking it 4 to 5 times a day. One user said her family started calling it "the traffic light."

## It sparked conversation, but not always action
My own experience: Magali asked me three times what she should do based on the light. Which is great! But also... three times in two months. The gap between awareness and behaviour change is real. Most people shifted their dishwasher or washing machine timing occasionally, but it wasn't transformative.

## Seb's mom never saw green
This was one of my favourite insights. She was super motivated but told us: "I'm never home when the light is green." Because cheap energy means midday, and midday means she is at work. It is a perfect illustration of why shifting consumption is harder than it sounds for people without home batteries or smart appliances.

## One of our testers is colorblind
William literally couldn't tell green from yellow. We should have thought of that. Noted for v2.

## The brutal honesty was the best part
One user said the light was so bright his wife thought aliens were coming. Another said it fell off the wall. Someone requested it also make coffee. (Noted.)

## The thing that made it all worth it
When we asked "based on your experience with the Energy Pebble, would you now switch to a dynamic contract?", the answer was yes. Even from people who had been skeptical. Even from William, who scored the device 2/5 for usefulness.

The device didn't need to be perfect. It just needed to make the invisible visible. Once people could *see* that prices fluctuate, the idea of a dynamic contract stopped being abstract and scary.

## What I learned personally
I never really share side projects publicly. But this one reminded me why building things is fun. We used 3D printing, soldering, open-source firmware, public APIs, user research: basically every tool in the shed. And the conversations we had with test users were worth more than any market report.

If you're sitting on a side project idea: just build it. It doesn't need to be polished. Ours literally fell off walls.
