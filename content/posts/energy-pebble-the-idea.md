A while ago, my friends William, Seb and I were having one of those evening conversations about energy. We all work in or around the energy sector, and we kept coming back to the same question: why do so few people have a dynamic energy contract?

In Belgium, energy prices change every 15 minutes on the wholesale market. With a dynamic contract you pay those real prices: cheap when there is lots of wind and sun, expensive during peak hours. The savings potential is real, but almost nobody does it.

I had a dynamic contract when I lived in Amsterdam, and I remember the struggle of trying to explain to Magali: "don't turn on the washing machine now, wait two hours!" She would look at me like I was losing it. And honestly, checking apps, interpreting graphs, remembering price curves... it is a lot to ask from someone who just wants clean clothes.

So we started thinking: what if the information was just *there*? No app, no graph, no explanation needed. Just a light on your wall.

Green: go ahead, energy is cheap and clean.
Red: maybe wait a bit.

That was the start of the [Energy Pebble](/projects/energy-pebble/).

We wanted to test a very specific hypothesis: if people can *see* the state of the grid at a glance, will they start shifting their consumption? And more importantly, would they then actually consider switching to a dynamic contract?

No pitch deck. No funding round. Just three people who wanted to know if a dumb-simple light could change behaviour.
